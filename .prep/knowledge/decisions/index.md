Rough first readings that settle are acceptable (user agreed).

* [Chord detection: exhaustive fret search with a common-shape prior](/decisions/01M7W8BZ7D602WM68NJERQ5V7J.md) - Score every per-string fret (0-12 or muted) whose fundamental is a strong peak by explained spectral peak weight, instead of a fixed voicing dictionary (misses uncommon voicings) or per-string poly estimation (built for near-open strings). Identical-sounding fingerings are broken by small penalties (muted, high frets) and a bonus for COMMON_UKULELE_SHAPES, since audio can't tell them apart.

* [Chord naming and theory live in TypeScript; WASM reports frets only](/decisions/01M7X3VYJ9506CARD0JB5WDKZM.md) - The chord namer and theory (names, alternatives, tone roles, spelling, key fit) move from tuner-core to web/src/lib/theory. tuner.wasm is at 158.7 of its 160 KB budget and name tables plus spelling would exceed it; theory is presentation-side and will grow (key detection, suggestions), and vitest makes it cheap to test. tuner-core keeps a pitch-set check only for chord_eval. Supersedes the in-core name_chord/Quality used by the wasm exports.
* [Systematic chord namer over every root, simplest reading wins](/decisions/01M7X3VYN2B2TEHR1WRC0ZJ3RF.md) - Instead of a fixed list of chord formulas, each candidate root (every sounding pitch class) is analysed into third, fifth, seventh, 6, extensions and alterations, the name is assembled from those parts and scored by complexity (alterations, adds, missing third, root not lowest); the lowest score is the name, the next ones are alternatives. Any combination of notes gets a name, which a formula list can't guarantee (user asked for 'pretty much any combination').
